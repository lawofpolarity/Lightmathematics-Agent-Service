# Intent, Hypotheses and Claim Boundaries

## Commercial intention

Test whether a buyer has a reason to purchase a bounded reliance inspection
before an AI agent acts on information.

The proposed purchased outcome is:

> Inspect whether this information is appropriate to rely upon for this declared
> operation, explain the governing evidence/state, and issue a machine-readable
> receipt.

## H1 — State-preservation hypothesis

Across transformations, mutations and handoffs, a governed LM implementation may
preserve decision-relevant state that a realistic comparison pipeline loses.

This remains unproven against the strongest fair comparator.

## H2 — Resolution hypothesis

Where required state is incomplete, LM may localize what is missing or unresolved
in a way that changes downstream agent behavior correctly.

This remains unproven as an LM-specific effect.

## H3 — Evidence-receipt hypothesis

A bounded decision plus traceable supporting state may have operational value to
agents/developers beyond a bare answer or generic confidence score.

Commercial willingness to pay remains untested.

## H0 — Conventional-equivalence hypothesis

An equivalently instrumented conventional provenance/governance/dependency/policy
stack can reproduce the same decisions, evidence and economics.

Stage 2A supports H0 for the minimal decision mapping. Further stages must attempt
to reject or retain H0 for the complete service.

## What success would mean

A meaningful favorable result requires more than LM producing the correct label.
The comparator must receive equivalent information/capability, and LM must show a
reproducible advantage in preservation/resolution, actionable evidence, or
economics attributable to a tested mechanism.

## What failure would mean

If strong conventional systems reproduce the complete decision and evidence at
comparable or lower cost, the differentiation claim contracts. LM mechanisms may
still have engineering, packaging, integration, research, or domain value.

## Prohibited inference

Do not infer from this benchmark alone that:
- LM makes models generally smarter;
- Sigma13 is universally privileged;
- LM is a new general reasoning architecture;
- commercial systems fail the benchmark before they are actually tested;
- synthetic normalized work equals production cost/latency;
- repository traffic equals willingness to pay;
- payment establishes truth or authority.
